## Definition and Classification

### Definition
Sexually transmitted diseases (STDs) are infections transmitted primarily through sexual contact, including vaginal, anal, and oral sex. They represent a significant public health concern due to their impact on reproductive health, transmission to newborns, and association with HIV.

### Classification by Causative Organisms

| Category | Examples | Key Characteristics |
|----------|----------|-------------------|
| **Bacterial** | Gonorrhea (Neisseria gonorrhoeae)<br>Syphilis (Treponema pallidum)<br>Chlamydia (Chlamydia trachomatis) | Often curable with antibiotics; can cause serious complications if untreated |
| **Viral** | HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus)<br>HSV (Herpes Simplex Virus)<br>HPV (Human Papillomavirus)<br>Hepatitis B | Chronic infections; some preventable by vaccination; lifelong management |
| **Parasitic** | Trichomoniasis (Trichomonas vaginalis)<br>Pubic lice (Phthirus pubis) | Treatable; often asymptomatic |
| **Fungal** | Candidiasis (Candida albicans) | Usually not sexually transmitted; opportunistic |

## Epidemiology

### Global Burden
- **WHO Estimates (2022)**: 1 million new STD cases daily
- **Most Common STDs Worldwide**:
  1. Chlamydia: ~131 million cases annually
  2. Gonorrhea: ~78 million cases
  3. Syphilis: ~6 million cases
  4. Trichomoniasis: ~156 million cases
- **HIV/AIDS**: 38.4 million people living with HIV (2021); 650,000 AIDS-related deaths

### Indian Context
- **HIV Prevalence**: 0.22% (NACO 2023) - 23.1 lakh people living with HIV
- **Regional Variations**:
  - Highest prevalence: Northeast (Nagaland: 1.5%, Manipur: 1.4%)
  - South India: Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana
  - Urban vs Rural: Higher in urban areas (23.5 lakh in urban vs 13.6 lakh rural)
- **Syphilis**: Rising trend, especially congenital syphilis (estimated 60,000 cases annually)
- **Gonorrhea/Chlamydia**: High burden among urban youth and high-risk groups
- **High-Risk Groups**: MSM (17% HIV prevalence), FSWs, IDUs, migrants, truckers

**Key Epidemiological Concepts**:
- **Incidence**: New cases per population per time (e.g., 30-40 million STD cases annually in India)
- **Prevalence**: Total existing cases (e.g., HIV prevalence 0.22%)
- **Endemic vs Epidemic**: STDs are endemic in India with localized epidemics in high-risk areas

**Table: STD Burden in India (Estimated Annual Cases)**

| STD | Estimated Cases | Key Affected Groups |
|-----|-----------------|-------------------|
| HIV | 23.1 lakh PLHIV | MSM, FSWs, IDUs |

## Transmission

### Modes of Transmission
1. **Sexual Contact**: Vaginal, anal, oral intercourse
2. **Vertical Transmission**: Mother-to-child during pregnancy/delivery/breastfeeding
3. **Blood-borne**: Sharing needles, contaminated blood products
4. **Other**: Rare non-sexual transmission (e.g., fomites for some parasites)

### Risk Factors

#### Biological Factors
- Age: Peak incidence 15-24 years
- Gender: Women more susceptible to complications
- Immunity: Compromised immunity increases severity
- Anatomy: Cervical ectopy in young women

#### Behavioral Factors
- Multiple sexual partners
- Unprotected sex (condom use only 5.2% consistent nationally)
- Early sexual debut
- Substance use impairing judgment

#### Social Determinants (Indian Context)
- **Stigma and Discrimination**: Deep-rooted cultural taboos around sexuality
- **Gender Inequalities**: Women bear disproportionate burden; male-dominated decision-making
- **Socioeconomic Factors**: Poverty, migration, low education
- **Cultural Practices**: Early marriage, preference for male children leading to sex-selective abortions
- **Urbanization**: Changing sexual behaviors, increased anonymity
- **Healthcare Access**: Rural-urban disparities; low health literacy

## Public Health Implications

### Community Medicine Perspective
- **Surveillance**: Integrated Disease Surveillance Programme (IDSP) monitors STD trends
- **Prevention Strategies**: ABC approach (Abstinence, Be faithful, Condoms) adapted for Indian culture
- **Population-Level Interventions**:
  - Targeted Interventions (TI) for high-risk groups
  - School-based comprehensive sexuality education
  - Workplace interventions for migrant workers
  - Community mobilization to reduce stigma

### Challenges in Indian Context
- **Stigma**: Prevents testing and treatment-seeking
- **Healthcare Access**: Shortage of trained providers in rural areas
- **Migration**: Interstate labor migration spreads infections
- **Condom Myths**: Cultural barriers, religious objections
- **MSM and Transgender**: Legal discrimination, social exclusion

---

## Key Facts for MBBS Students
- STDs are preventable through behavior change and vaccination
- Early diagnosis and treatment prevent complications
- Partner notification is crucial for control
- Community-based approaches are essential in resource-limited settings
- Integration with HIV programs enhances effectiveness
