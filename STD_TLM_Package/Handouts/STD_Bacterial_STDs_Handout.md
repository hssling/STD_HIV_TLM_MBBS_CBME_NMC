## Gonorrhea

### Etiology
- **Causative Agent**: Neisseria gonorrhoeae (Gram-negative diplococcus)
- **Transmission**: Sexual contact (vaginal, anal, oral); vertical transmission possible
- **Incubation Period**: 2-7 days (range 1-14 days)

### Epidemiology (Indian Context)
- Estimated 2-3 million cases annually
- Higher prevalence in urban areas and high-risk groups
- Increasing antimicrobial resistance (cefixime, ciprofloxacin)
- Co-infection with chlamydia in 20-30% cases

### Pathophysiology
- Primarily infects mucosal surfaces (urethra, cervix, rectum, pharynx)
- Ascending infection can lead to PID, epididymitis
- Disseminated gonococcal infection (DGI) in 1-3% cases
- Immune evasion through antigenic variation

### Clinical Features

**Males**:
- Acute urethritis: Profuse yellow-green purulent discharge, dysuria, frequency
- Complications: Epididymitis, prostatitis, urethral stricture

**Females**:
- Often asymptomatic (50-70%)
- Mucopurulent cervicitis, abnormal vaginal discharge
- Complications: PID (20-40% risk), infertility, ectopic pregnancy

**Other Sites**:
- Pharyngitis (asymptomatic in 90%)
- Proctitis (rectal pain, discharge)
- Conjunctivitis (neonatal)

### Complications
- **Reproductive**: PID, infertility, ectopic pregnancy, chronic pelvic pain
- **Systemic**: DGI (arthritis, tenosynovitis, dermatitis), endocarditis, meningitis
- **Neonatal**: Ophthalmia neonatorum, sepsis

### Diagnosis
- **Microscopy**: Gram stain (intracellular GN diplococci)
- **Culture**: Thayer-Martin medium; antibiotic sensitivity testing
- **NAAT**: Nucleic acid amplification tests (PCR) - most sensitive
- **Window Period**: Detectable immediately after infection

### Treatment (NACO Guidelines 2020)
- **Uncomplicated**: Ceftriaxone 500mg IM single dose + Azithromycin 1g PO single dose
- **Complicated**: Ceftriaxone 1g IV/IM daily for 7-14 days
- **Test of Cure**: Recommended 7-14 days post-treatment
- **Partner Treatment**: Essential to prevent reinfection

## Syphilis

### Etiology
- **Causative Agent**: Treponema pallidum (spirochete)
- **Transmission**: Sexual contact, vertical (congenital), blood-borne
- **Incubation Period**: 10-90 days (average 21 days)

### Epidemiology (Indian Context)
- Estimated 60,000 congenital syphilis cases annually
- Rising trend in adult syphilis, especially among high-risk groups
- Regional variations: Higher in Northeast and urban areas
- Co-infection with HIV increases transmission and progression

### Pathophysiology
- Multi-stage disease with systemic dissemination
- Immune response causes tissue damage and vasculitis
- Latency periods with potential reactivation
- Tertiary stage: Gummatous lesions, cardiovascular, neurological involvement

### Clinical Features

**Primary Syphilis (Chancre)**:
- Painless ulcer at inoculation site (genitals, anus, mouth)
- Clean base, indurated borders, cartilaginous feel
- Regional lymphadenopathy (rubbery, non-tender)
- Heals spontaneously in 3-6 weeks

**Secondary Syphilis**:
- Generalized rash (maculopapular, palms/soles involved)
- Condylomata lata (moist warts in skin folds)
- Systemic symptoms: Fever, malaise, lymphadenopathy
- Mucous patches, alopecia

**Latent Syphilis**:
- Asymptomatic; seropositive
- Early latent (<1 year), Late latent (>1 year)

**Tertiary Syphilis**:
- Cardiovascular: Aortic aneurysm, aortic regurgitation
- Neurosyphilis: Tabes dorsalis, general paresis
- Gummas: Destructive lesions in skin/bone

**Congenital Syphilis**:
- Early: Rash, rhinitis, hepatosplenomegaly
- Late: Interstitial keratitis, Hutchinson teeth, saddle nose

### Complications
- Neurological: Meningitis, stroke, dementia
- Cardiovascular: Aortic aneurysm, coronary ostial stenosis
- Fetal: Miscarriage, stillbirth, congenital defects
- Social: Stigma, treatment challenges

### Diagnosis
- **Dark Field Microscopy**: Direct visualization of spirochetes
- **Serological Tests**:
  - Non-treponemal: VDRL, RPR (quantitative, monitor treatment)
  - Treponemal: TPHA, FTA-ABS (confirmatory, remain positive)
- **CSF Analysis**: For neurosyphilis
- **Window Period**: 4-6 weeks for serological tests

### Treatment (NACO Guidelines 2020)
- **Primary/Secondary**: Benzathine penicillin 2.4 MU IM single dose
- **Latent**: Benzathine penicillin 2.4 MU IM weekly × 3 doses
- **Tertiary/Neurosyphilis**: Aqueous penicillin G 3-4 MU IV q4h × 14 days
- **Pregnancy**: Penicillin G (safe in all trimesters)
- **Alternative**: Doxycycline 100mg PO twice daily × 14 days
- **Jarisch-Herxheimer Reaction**: Monitor for fever/chills post-treatment

## Chlamydia

### Etiology
- **Causative Agent**: Chlamydia trachomatis (obligate intracellular bacterium)
- **Transmission**: Sexual contact; vertical transmission
- **Incubation Period**: 7-21 days

### Epidemiology (Indian Context)
- Estimated 5-6 million cases annually
- Most common bacterial STD worldwide
- Often asymptomatic (70-80% in women, 50% in men)
- High prevalence among young adults and high-risk groups

### Pathophysiology
- Infects columnar epithelium (cervix, urethra, rectum)
- Intracellular replication in inclusion bodies
- Chronic inflammation leads to scarring and fibrosis
- Serovars D-K cause genital infections; L1-L3 cause lymphogranuloma venereum

### Clinical Features

**Females**:
- Often asymptomatic
- Mucopurulent cervicitis, post-coital bleeding
- Complications: PID, infertility, ectopic pregnancy, chronic pelvic pain

**Males**:
- Urethritis: Clear discharge, mild dysuria
- Epididymitis: Scrotal pain, swelling
- Proctitis (in MSM)

**Other Manifestations**:
- Reactive arthritis (Reiter's syndrome)
- Lymphogranuloma venereum (inguinal syndrome)
- Neonatal conjunctivitis/pneumonia

### Complications
- **Reproductive**: Tubal infertility, ectopic pregnancy
- **Perinatal**: Preterm labor, low birth weight
- **Systemic**: Reactive arthritis, conjunctivitis
- **Cancer Risk**: Possible association with cervical cancer

### Diagnosis
- **NAAT**: Most sensitive (urine, swabs) - first-line test
- **Culture**: McCoy cells, less commonly used
- **Serology**: For lymphogranuloma venereum
- **Window Period**: Detectable 1-2 weeks post-infection

### Treatment (NACO Guidelines 2020)
- **Uncomplicated**: Azithromycin 1g PO single dose OR Doxycycline 100mg PO twice daily × 7 days
- **Complicated**: Doxycycline 100mg PO twice daily × 14-21 days
- **Pregnancy**: Azithromycin preferred (doxycycline contraindicated)
- **Test of Cure**: Recommended for high-risk cases or persistent symptoms
- **Partner Treatment**: Essential

## Public Health Aspects

### Surveillance and Control
- **Integrated Case Reporting**: Through IDSP and NACO
- **Sentinel Surveillance**: STI clinics monitor trends and resistance
- **Partner Notification**: Voluntary and provider-assisted
- **Contact Tracing**: Essential for syphilis and gonorrhea

### Prevention Strategies (Indian Context)
- **Condom Promotion**: Social marketing through NACO
- **Screening Programs**: Targeted for high-risk groups
- **Health Education**: Community awareness campaigns
- **Antimicrobial Resistance Monitoring**: National surveillance network

### Challenges in India
- **Asymptomatic Infections**: Undetected reservoir
- **Drug Resistance**: Emerging gonococcal resistance
- **Stigma**: Prevents testing and treatment
- **Healthcare Access**: Rural-urban disparities

---

## Key Facts for MBBS Students
- Bacterial STDs are curable but complications are preventable
- Asymptomatic infections contribute significantly to transmission
- Partner treatment is crucial to prevent reinfection
- Antimicrobial resistance requires updated treatment guidelines
- Integration with HIV programs enhances control efforts
