## HIV/AIDS

### Etiology
- **Causative Agent**: Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV-1 and HIV-2)
- **Structure**: Retrovirus with envelope, core, and genetic material
- **Transmission**: Sexual (80%), blood-borne (15%), vertical (5%)
- **Incubation Period**: 2-4 weeks (seroconversion); 8-10 years to AIDS

### Epidemiology (Indian Context)
- **Prevalence**: 0.22% (23.1 lakh PLHIV, NACO 2023)
- **New Infections**: ~50,000 annually (66% decline since 2007)
- **Regional Distribution**: Highest in South India and Northeast
- **High-Risk Groups**: MSM (17%), FSWs (2.4%), IDUs (7.1%), migrants
- **ART Coverage**: 80% of PLHIV on treatment; 90% viral suppression

### Pathophysiology
- **Viral Entry**: CD4+ T-cells via CD4 receptor and co-receptors (CCR5/CXCR4)
- **Replication**: Reverse transcription → Integration → Viral production
- **Immune Response**: CD4 depletion → Immune dysfunction → Opportunistic infections
- **Natural History**: Acute infection → Asymptomatic → Symptomatic → AIDS

### Clinical Features

**Acute HIV Infection (Seroconversion)**:
- Fever, rash, lymphadenopathy, pharyngitis
- CD4 count 200-500 cells/μL
- High viral load (10^5-10^7 copies/mL)

**Asymptomatic Stage**:
- No symptoms; CD4 count >500 cells/μL
- Viral replication continues
- Duration: 8-10 years untreated

**Symptomatic HIV**:
- Persistent generalized lymphadenopathy
- Weight loss, fever, night sweats
- Oral thrush, herpes zoster

**AIDS**:
- CD4 <200 cells/μL
- Opportunistic infections: TB, PCP, cryptococcal meningitis
- Malignancies: Kaposi sarcoma, lymphoma

### Complications
- **Opportunistic Infections**: TB (common in India), Pneumocystis, Cryptococcus
- **Malignancies**: Kaposi sarcoma, non-Hodgkin lymphoma
- **Neurological**: HIV encephalopathy, peripheral neuropathy
- **Cardiovascular**: Increased risk of heart disease
- **Social**: Stigma, discrimination, mental health issues

### Diagnosis
- **Screening**: ELISA (antibody detection)
- **Confirmation**: Western blot or immunofluorescence
- **Viral Load**: Quantitative PCR (treatment monitoring)
- **CD4 Count**: Immune status assessment
- **Window Period**: 4-6 weeks; nucleic acid testing reduces to 7-10 days

### Treatment and Prevention
- **ART**: Tenofovir + Lamivudine + Efavirenz (TLE) or Dolutegravir (TLD)
- **PrEP**: Tenofovir/FTC for high-risk individuals
- **PEP**: Post-exposure prophylaxis within 72 hours
- **Vaccination**: No vaccine available; research ongoing

## Herpes Simplex Virus (HSV)

### Etiology
- **Types**: HSV-1 (oral) and HSV-2 (genital)
- **Transmission**: Direct contact with lesions or secretions
- **Incubation Period**: 2-12 days

### Epidemiology (Indian Context)
- **HSV-2 Prevalence**: 10-15 million infected adults
- **Primary Infection**: Often asymptomatic or mild
- **Recurrence**: HSV-2 recurs more frequently than HSV-1
- **HIV Co-infection**: Increases HSV reactivation and transmission

### Pathophysiology
- **Primary Infection**: Viral replication in epithelial cells → Vesicles
- **Latency**: Virus establishes in sensory ganglia (trigeminal for HSV-1, sacral for HSV-2)
- **Reactivation**: Stress, immunosuppression triggers recurrence
- **Immune Response**: Cell-mediated immunity controls but doesn't eliminate virus

### Clinical Features

**Primary Genital Herpes**:
- Painful vesicles/ulcers on genitals
- Fever, malaise, inguinal lymphadenopathy
- Duration: 2-3 weeks
- Severe in immunocompromised patients

**Recurrent Genital Herpes**:
- Prodrome: Tingling, itching 24-48 hours before lesions
- Fewer lesions, milder symptoms
- Frequency: 4-6 episodes/year initially, decreases over time

**Atypical Presentations**:
- Asymptomatic shedding (transmission source)
- Proctitis in MSM
- Neonatal herpes (severe, disseminated)

### Complications
- **Neonatal**: Disseminated infection, encephalitis
- **Immunocompromised**: Severe, prolonged episodes
- **Psychosocial**: Anxiety, depression, sexual dysfunction
- **HIV Association**: Genital ulcers increase HIV transmission 2-3 fold

### Diagnosis
- **Clinical**: Characteristic vesicles/ulcers
- **PCR**: Most sensitive (swab from lesions)
- **Serology**: Type-specific antibodies (HSV-1 vs HSV-2)
- **Tzanck Smear**: Multinucleated giant cells
- **Viral Culture**: Less commonly used now

### Treatment and Prevention
- **Acute Episodes**: Acyclovir 400mg PO 5×/day × 7-10 days
- **Suppressive Therapy**: Acyclovir 400mg PO twice daily
- **Prevention**: Condoms, antiviral therapy during pregnancy
- **Vaccination**: No vaccine available

## Human Papillomavirus (HPV)

### Etiology
- **Types**: >200 types; 40+ affect anogenital tract
- **Transmission**: Sexual contact, autoinoculation
- **Incubation Period**: 1-8 months (average 3 months)

### Epidemiology (Indian Context)
- **Prevalence**: 20-25 million infected women
- **Cervical Cancer**: 2nd most common cancer in Indian women
- **High-Risk Types**: 16, 18 cause 70% of cervical cancers
- **Vaccination Coverage**: Low; introduced in some states

### Pathophysiology
- **Infection**: Basal epithelial cells
- **Replication**: Viral DNA integrates into host genome
- **Transformation**: E6/E7 oncoproteins inactivate p53/Rb
- **Progression**: CIN → Carcinoma in situ → Invasive cancer

### Clinical Features

**Low-Risk HPV (6,11)**:
- Genital warts (condylomata acuminata)
- Cauliflower-like lesions on genitals
- Usually asymptomatic

**High-Risk HPV (16,18,31,33,45)**:
- Often asymptomatic
- Cervical dysplasia (CIN 1-3)
- Cervical cancer
- Anal, penile, oropharyngeal cancers

**Other Manifestations**:
- Recurrent respiratory papillomatosis (rare)
- Bowenoid papulosis

### Complications
- **Cervical Cancer**: Leading cause of cancer death in Indian women
- **Other Cancers**: Anal, vulvar, penile, oropharyngeal
- **Pregnancy**: Increased risk of preterm delivery
- **Transmission**: High during active infection

### Diagnosis
- **Clinical**: Visual inspection of warts
- **Pap Smear**: Cervical cytology screening
- **HPV DNA Test**: High-risk HPV detection
- **Colposcopy**: Directed biopsy for abnormalities
- **Histopathology**: CIN grading

### Treatment and Prevention
- **Warts**: Cryotherapy, podophyllin, surgical removal
- **Dysplasia**: LEEP, cone biopsy
- **Vaccination**: Cervarix (2v) or Gardasil (4v/9v) at 9-26 years
- **Screening**: Pap smear every 3 years (21-65 years)
- **Prevention**: Condoms reduce but don't eliminate transmission

## Hepatitis B

### Etiology
- **Causative Agent**: Hepatitis B Virus (DNA virus)
- **Transmission**: Sexual (30-60%), blood-borne, vertical
- **Incubation Period**: 30-180 days (average 75 days)

### Epidemiology (Indian Context)
- **Carrier Rate**: 3-4% (40 million chronic carriers)
- **Viral Hepatitis Burden**: 40,000 deaths annually
- **Regional Variation**: Higher in Northeast and tribal areas
- **Vaccination Coverage**: 85% through national program

### Pathophysiology
- **Acute Infection**: Immune-mediated hepatocyte damage
- **Chronic Infection**: Immune tolerance in perinatal acquisition
- **Cirrhosis**: Fibrosis from chronic inflammation
- **HCC**: Direct oncogenic effect of HBx protein

### Clinical Features

**Acute Hepatitis B**:
- Fever, fatigue, jaundice, abdominal pain
- Elevated ALT/AST, bilirubin
- Self-limiting in 90% adults

**Chronic Hepatitis B**:
- Often asymptomatic
- Progressive to cirrhosis (20-30% over 20 years)
- Hepatocellular carcinoma risk (100-200× increased)

**Fulminant Hepatitis**:
- Rare (<1%); high mortality
- Massive liver necrosis

### Complications
- **Cirrhosis**: Portal hypertension, variceal bleeding
- **Hepatocellular Carcinoma**: Leading cancer in India
- **Extrahepatic**: Polyarteritis nodosa, glomerulonephritis
- **Vertical Transmission**: Chronic infection in 90% infants

### Diagnosis
- **HBsAg**: Marker of infection (acute/chronic)
- **Anti-HBs**: Immunity (vaccine or resolved infection)
- **Anti-HBc**: Past/ongoing infection
- **HBeAg**: High infectivity marker
- **HBV DNA**: Quantitative viral load
- **Liver Function Tests**: ALT, bilirubin, albumin

### Treatment and Prevention
- **Acute**: Supportive care; monitor for fulminant disease
- **Chronic**: Tenofovir or entecavir (oral antivirals)
- **Vaccination**: 3-dose series (0,1,6 months); birth dose for infants
- **Prevention**: Safe sex, screening blood donors, HBIG for exposure
- **Screening**: High-risk groups (healthcare workers, MSM, IDUs)

## Public Health Aspects

### Viral STD Syndromic Management
- **Genital Ulcer Disease**: HSV, syphilis, chancroid
- **Urethral Discharge**: HSV possible in recurrent cases
- **Prevention Integration**: Combined HIV/STD prevention programs

### Challenges in India
- **Co-infections**: HIV-HBV common in high-risk groups
- **Stigma**: Prevents testing for viral STDs
- **Access to Care**: ART centers provide integrated services
- **Vaccine Coverage**: HPV vaccination needs expansion

### Key Prevention Strategies
- **Vaccination Programs**: HBV universal; HPV targeted
- **Harm Reduction**: Needle exchange for IDUs
- **Condom Promotion**: Dual protection (STI + pregnancy)
- **Screening**: Integrated HIV-STD testing

---

## Key Facts for MBBS Students
- Viral STDs are chronic but manageable with treatment
- Vaccination prevents HPV and HBV infections
- Early HIV diagnosis and ART prevent progression
- Genital herpes increases HIV transmission risk
- Integration of services improves outcomes
