### [Section 2: Types and Classification - 7:00-12:00]
*[Visual: Animated classification wheel, clinical images, pathogen illustrations]*

**Narrator:** "STDs are classified by their causative organisms, each with distinct characteristics, transmission patterns, and management approaches."

#### Bacterial STDs
**Narrator:** "Bacterial STDs are curable with antibiotics but can cause severe complications if untreated. They include gonorrhea, syphilis, and chlamydia."

*[Clinical Images: Gram stains, culture plates, anatomical diagrams]*
- **Gonorrhea:** Neisseria gonorrhoeae - Gram-negative diplococcus
- **Syphilis:** Treponema pallidum - Spirochete
- **Chlamydia:** Chlamydia trachomatis - Obligate intracellular bacterium

#### Viral STDs
**Narrator:** "Viral STDs are chronic infections requiring long-term management. They include HIV, HSV, HPV, and Hepatitis B."

*[Visual: Virus structures, lifecycle animations]*
- **HIV:** Retrovirus causing immune deficiency
- **HSV:** Herpes simplex virus types 1 and 2
- **HPV:** Human papillomavirus - 200+ types
- **Hepatitis B:** DNA virus causing liver disease

#### Parasitic and Fungal STDs
**Narrator:** "Parasitic STDs like trichomoniasis and pubic lice are treatable but often recurrent. Candidiasis is usually opportunistic."

*[Microscopic images, life cycle diagrams]*
- **Trichomoniasis:** Trichomonas vaginalis
- **Pubic Lice:** Phthirus pubis
- **Candidiasis:** Candida albicans

**On Screen Text:** "Classification determines diagnosis and treatment strategies"

### [Section 3: Etiology - 12:00-17:00]
*[Visual: Pathogen details, transmission animations, risk factor graphics]*

**Narrator:** "Understanding the causative agents is fundamental to effective STD management."

#### Bacterial Pathogens
**Narrator:** "Bacterial STDs are caused by specific organisms with unique characteristics."

- **Gonorrhea:** Neisseria gonorrhoeae - Fastidious, requires special culture media
- **Syphilis:** Treponema pallidum - Cannot be cultured in vitro, diagnosed serologically
- **Chlamydia:** Chlamydia trachomatis - Has unique biphasic lifecycle

#### Viral Pathogens
**Narrator:** "Viral STDs involve complex interactions with the immune system."

- **HIV:** Retrovirus integrating into host DNA, causing progressive immune depletion
- **HSV:** Establishes latency in sensory ganglia, reactivates with triggers
- **HPV:** Epithelial tropism, integrates into host genome causing oncogenic transformation
- **HBV:** Hepadnavirus with high mutation rate, causes chronic liver disease

#### Other Pathogens
**Narrator:** "Parasitic and fungal agents complete the STD spectrum."

- **Trichomonas vaginalis:** Flagellated protozoan, causes vaginitis
- **Phthirus pubis:** Ectoparasite, blood-feeding louse
- **Candida albicans:** Opportunistic yeast, normal flora becoming pathogenic

**Visual Suggestion:** Microscopic images with annotations, 3D animations of pathogen structures

*[Interactive Element: Virtual microscope activity - identify pathogens from images]*

**Speaker Notes:** Emphasize how etiology guides laboratory diagnosis and treatment selection.

### [Section 5: Transmission and Incubation Period - 25:00-30:00]
*[Visual: Transmission route animations, timeline graphics, prevention demonstrations]*

**Narrator:** "Understanding transmission dynamics and incubation periods is crucial for prevention and early intervention."

#### Modes of Transmission
**Narrator:** "STDs transmit through various routes, requiring comprehensive prevention approaches."

- **Sexual Contact:** Vaginal, anal, oral intercourse (primary route)
- **Vertical Transmission:** Mother-to-child during pregnancy/delivery/breastfeeding
- **Blood-borne:** Sharing needles, contaminated blood products
- **Other:** Rare non-sexual transmission (fomites for parasites)

#### Incubation Periods
**Narrator:** "Incubation periods vary widely, affecting diagnosis and contact tracing."

**Timeline Display:**
- **Gonorrhea:** 2-7 days (range 1-14 days)
- **Syphilis:** 10-90 days (average 21 days)
- **Chlamydia:** 7-21 days
- **HIV:** 2-4 weeks (seroconversion); 8-10 years to AIDS
- **HSV:** 2-12 days
- **HPV:** 1-8 months (average 3 months)
- **HBV:** 30-180 days (average 75 days)

#### Risk Factors
**Narrator:** "Multiple factors increase transmission probability."

- **Biological:** Age (15-24 peak), gender, immune status
- **Behavioral:** Multiple partners, unprotected sex, substance use
- **Social:** Migration, poverty, low education
- **Healthcare:** Untreated infections, poor access to services

**Visual Suggestion:** Animated transmission cycles, risk factor pyramids

*[Interactive Element: Transmission chain game - trace how infection spreads through population]*

**Speaker Notes:** Emphasize community-level transmission prevention through behavior change and structural interventions.

### [Section 6: Pathophysiology - 30:00-35:00]
*[Visual: Anatomical diagrams, pathophysiological animations, cellular interactions]*

**Narrator:** "Pathophysiology explains how pathogens cause disease and guides treatment approaches."

#### Bacterial STD Pathophysiology
**Narrator:** "Bacterial STDs cause local and systemic inflammation with potential for chronic complications."

- **Gonorrhea:** Mucosal invasion, ascending infection, immune evasion through variation
- **Syphilis:** Multi-stage disease, vasculitis, immune complex deposition
- **Chlamydia:** Intracellular replication, chronic inflammation, scarring

#### Viral STD Pathophysiology
**Narrator:** "Viral STDs involve complex host-pathogen interactions and immune responses."

- **HIV:** CD4 depletion, immune dysfunction, opportunistic infections
- **HSV:** Latency in ganglia, reactivation triggers, neuronal damage
- **HPV:** Epithelial transformation, oncogene activation, immune evasion
- **HBV:** Hepatocyte damage, fibrosis, oncogenic potential

#### Host Response
**Narrator:** "The immune response determines disease progression and outcomes."

- **Innate Immunity:** Initial recognition and containment
- **Adaptive Immunity:** Specific antibody and cell-mediated responses
- **Immunopathology:** Tissue damage from excessive inflammation
- **Immune Evasion:** Pathogen strategies to avoid clearance

**Visual Suggestion:** Multi-step pathophysiological flowcharts, cellular animations

### [Section 7: Clinical Features - 35:00-43:00]
*[Visual: Clinical images (with consent), symptom animations, patient scenarios]*

**Narrator:** "Clinical presentation varies by STD type, stage, and individual factors."

#### Bacterial STDs - Clinical Presentation
**Narrator:** "Bacterial STDs often present with acute symptoms but can be asymptomatic."

**Gonorrhea:**
- Males: Purulent urethral discharge, dysuria, epididymitis
- Females: Cervicitis, PID, infertility complications
- Complications: DGI, neonatal ophthalmia

**Syphilis:**
- Primary: Painless chancre, regional lymphadenopathy
- Secondary: Rash, mucous patches, condylomata lata
- Tertiary: Cardiovascular, neurological, gummatous lesions

**Chlamydia:**
- Females: Asymptomatic (70-80%), mucopurulent cervicitis
- Males: Urethritis, epididymitis, reactive arthritis
- Complications: PID, ectopic pregnancy, chronic pelvic pain

#### Viral STDs - Clinical Presentation
**Narrator:** "Viral STDs may have acute and chronic phases with varying severity."

**HIV:**
- Acute: Fever, rash, lymphadenopathy, seroconversion illness
- Chronic: Asymptomatic, then opportunistic infections
- AIDS: CD4 <200, severe immunosuppression

**HSV:**
- Primary: Painful genital ulcers, systemic symptoms
- Recurrent: Prodromal tingling, clustered vesicles
- Atypical: Asymptomatic shedding, proctitis

**HPV:**
- Low-risk: Genital warts, usually asymptomatic
- High-risk: Cervical dysplasia, cancer precursors
- Other: Anal, penile, oropharyngeal lesions

**HBV:**
- Acute: Jaundice, fatigue, abdominal pain
- Chronic: Asymptomatic carrier state, cirrhosis, HCC
- Fulminant: Rare but life-threatening

**Visual Suggestion:** Side-by-side comparison charts, clinical photography with annotations

### [Section 8: Complications - 43:00-48:00]
*[Visual: Complication diagrams, outcome statistics, prevention graphics]*

**Narrator:** "Untreated STDs lead to serious complications affecting individual and community health."

#### Reproductive Complications
**Narrator:** "STDs significantly impact reproductive health and family planning."

- **PID and Infertility:** Chlamydia, gonorrhea cause tubal scarring
- **Ectopic Pregnancy:** Increased risk with PID history
- **Chronic Pelvic Pain:** Long-term sequela of untreated infections
- **Neonatal Complications:** Ophthalmia, pneumonia, sepsis

#### Systemic Complications
**Narrator:** "Some STDs cause widespread organ system involvement."

- **HIV:** Opportunistic infections, malignancies, neurological disease
- **Syphilis:** Cardiovascular (aortic aneurysm), neurological (tabes dorsalis)
- **HBV:** Cirrhosis, hepatocellular carcinoma, extrahepatic manifestations
- **HSV:** Neonatal herpes, disseminated infection

#### Social and Economic Impact
**Narrator:** "Complications extend beyond health to social and economic spheres."

- **Stigma and Discrimination:** Affects mental health and healthcare access
- **Lost Productivity:** Illness, hospitalization, premature death
- **Healthcare Costs:** Treatment of complications vs prevention
- **Intergenerational Transmission:** Vertical transmission impacts future generations

**Visual Suggestion:** Complication wheel diagram, burden of disease statistics

### [Section 9: Diagnosis - 48:00-55:00]
*[Visual: Laboratory demonstrations, diagnostic algorithms, testing workflows]*

**Narrator:** "Accurate diagnosis combines clinical assessment, laboratory tests, and epidemiological information."

#### Clinical Assessment
**Narrator:** "History taking and physical examination form the foundation of diagnosis."

- **Sexual History:** 5 Ps approach (Partners, Practices, Protection, Past STDs, Pregnancy)
- **Risk Assessment:** Behavioral and biological factors
- **Physical Examination:** Genital, systemic, lymph node evaluation
- **Partner Evaluation:** Essential for bacterial STDs

#### Laboratory Diagnosis
**Narrator:** "Laboratory tests confirm diagnosis and guide treatment."

**Microscopy:**
- Gram stain (gonorrhea), wet mount (trichomonas), Tzanck smear (HSV)

**Culture:**
- Thayer-Martin media (gonorrhea), McCoy cells (chlamydia)

**Molecular Tests:**
- NAAT/PCR (chlamydia, gonorrhea, HSV) - gold standard
- Viral load (HIV, HBV)

**Serological Tests:**
- VDRL/TPHA (syphilis), ELISA/Western blot (HIV)
- Type-specific antibodies (HSV), HBsAg (HBV)

**Other:**
- Colposcopy (HPV), liver biopsy (HBV), CSF analysis (neurosyphilis)

#### Diagnostic Algorithms
**Narrator:** "Syndromic management guides diagnosis in resource-limited settings."

- **Urethral Discharge:** Presumptive gonorrhea/chlamydia treatment
- **Genital Ulcer:** Multiple possibilities - syphilis, HSV, chancroid
- **Vaginal Discharge:** Bacterial vaginosis, trichomoniasis, candidiasis
- **Lower Abdominal Pain:** PID evaluation

**Visual Suggestion:** Diagnostic flowchart animations, laboratory technique videos

*[Interactive Element: Diagnostic decision tree - navigate through symptoms to diagnosis]*

**Speaker Notes:** Emphasize integration of laboratory and clinical diagnosis in Indian healthcare system.

### [Section 10: Treatment - 55:00-62:00]
*[Visual: Treatment algorithms, medication demonstrations, follow-up protocols]*

**Narrator:** "Treatment follows evidence-based guidelines adapted to Indian context and resource availability."

#### Bacterial STD Treatment (NACO Guidelines 2020)
**Narrator:** "Bacterial STDs are curable with appropriate antibiotic therapy."

**Gonorrhea:**
- Uncomplicated: Ceftriaxone 500mg IM + Azithromycin 1g PO single dose
- Complicated: Ceftriaxone 1g IV/IM daily × 7-14 days
- Test of cure: Recommended 7-14 days post-treatment

**Syphilis:**
- Primary/Secondary: Benzathine penicillin 2.4 MU IM single dose
- Latent: Benzathine penicillin 2.4 MU IM weekly × 3 doses
- Neurosyphilis: Aqueous penicillin G 3-4 MU IV q4h × 14 days

**Chlamydia:**
- Uncomplicated: Azithromycin 1g PO single dose OR Doxycycline 100mg PO twice daily × 7 days
- Complicated: Doxycycline 100mg PO twice daily × 14-21 days

#### Viral STD Treatment
**Narrator:** "Viral STDs require long-term management and adherence support."

**HIV (NACO 2023):**
- First-line: TLD (Tenofovir + Lamivudine + Dolutegravir)
- Alternative: TLE (Tenofovir + Lamivudine + Efavirenz)
- Monitoring: Viral load at 6 months, annually thereafter

**HSV:**
- Acute: Acyclovir 400mg PO three times daily × 7-10 days
- Suppressive: Acyclovir 400mg PO twice daily for recurrent prevention

**HBV:**
- Chronic: Tenofovir or entecavir oral daily
- Acute: Supportive care, monitor for fulminant disease

#### Treatment Principles
**Narrator:** "Key principles ensure treatment effectiveness."

- **Partner Treatment:** Essential for bacterial STDs
- **Adherence Support:** Counseling and follow-up for chronic infections
- **Drug Resistance Monitoring:** Regular surveillance
- **Pregnancy Considerations:** Safe medications, vertical transmission prevention

**Visual Suggestion:** Treatment flowchart, medication administration videos

*[Interactive Element: Treatment planner - select STD and get regimen recommendations]*

**Speaker Notes:** Stress rational antibiotic use and adherence - critical for resistance prevention.

### [Section 11: Control and Prevention - 62:00-70:00]
*[Visual: Prevention campaigns, condom demonstrations, vaccination programs]*

**Narrator:** "Prevention requires multi-level approaches addressing individual, community, and structural factors."

#### Primary Prevention
**Narrator:** "Primary prevention stops STD acquisition through health promotion and protection."

- **ABC Approach:** Abstinence, Be faithful, Condoms
- **Vaccination:** HBV universal, HPV targeted, HIV research ongoing
- **PrEP:** Pre-exposure prophylaxis for high-risk individuals
- **Safe Sex Practices:** Consistent condom use, STI screening

#### Secondary Prevention
**Narrator:** "Secondary prevention focuses on early detection and treatment."

- **Screening Programs:** High-risk groups, antenatal care
- **Contact Tracing:** Partner notification and treatment
- **Regular Testing:** For sexually active individuals
- **Early Intervention:** Prevents complications and transmission

#### Tertiary Prevention
**Narrator:** "Tertiary prevention manages complications and prevents further deterioration."

- **ART Programs:** Viral suppression prevents transmission
- **Support Services:** Counseling, nutritional support
- **Rehabilitation:** For advanced disease complications
- **Harm Reduction:** Needle exchange, opioid substitution

#### Community-Level Interventions
**Narrator:** "Community medicine emphasizes population-based approaches."

- **IEC Campaigns:** Mass media, school programs, community mobilization
- **Targeted Interventions:** For high-risk groups (MSM, FSWs, IDUs)
- **Workplace Programs:** For migrant workers and uniformed services
- **Structural Interventions:** Legal reforms, economic empowerment

**Visual Suggestion:** Prevention pyramid diagram, community intervention examples

*[Interactive Element: Prevention strategy builder - combine interventions for maximum impact]*

**Speaker Notes:** Emphasize cost-effectiveness of prevention vs treatment - public health economics.
