## Section 2: Etiology (5:00 - 10:00)

**Visual Suggestion:** Animated viral structure with labels. Show electron microscope images of HIV.

**On-Screen Text:**  
- Causative Agent: HIV-1 and HIV-2  
- Viral Structure: Envelope, Core, Genome  
- Key Facts: Targets CD4+ cells, uses reverse transcriptase  

**Speaker Notes:**  
"HIV, or Human Immunodeficiency Virus, is a retrovirus from the Lentivirus family. There are two main types: HIV-1, which is more common and virulent worldwide, and HIV-2, found mainly in West Africa with slower progression. The virus has an envelope with gp120 and gp41 proteins, a core containing enzymes like reverse transcriptase, and a genome of nine genes. HIV specifically targets CD4+ T lymphocytes, macrophages, and dendritic cells, using CD4 receptors and co-receptors CCR5 or CXCR4 for entry. The reverse transcriptase enzyme converts viral RNA to DNA, which integrates into the host genome."

**Technical Directions:**

## Section 4: Transmission (15:00 - 20:00)

**Visual Suggestion:** Animated transmission scenarios (sexual contact, needle sharing, MTCT). Prevention icons.

**On-Screen Text:**  
- Primary Modes: Sexual, Blood-borne, MTCT  
- Risk Factors: Biological, Behavioral, Social  
- Prevention: U=U, Condoms, ART  

**Speaker Notes:**  
"HIV transmits primarily through unprotected sexual intercourse, sharing contaminated needles, and from mother to child. Sexual transmission is most common, especially with multiple partners or concurrent STIs. Blood-borne transmission occurs via needle-sharing or unscreened blood transfusions. Mother-to-child transmission happens during pregnancy, delivery, or breastfeeding, but ART reduces this to less than 2%. Risk factors include genital ulcers, inconsistent condom use, and social factors like migration. Remember U=U: Undetectable equals Untransmittable, meaning suppressed viral load prevents sexual transmission."

## Section 5: Incubation Period (20:00 - 23:00)

**Visual Suggestion:** Timeline graphic showing HIV progression phases.

**On-Screen Text:**  
- Window Period: 2-12 weeks  
- Phases: Acute, Chronic, AIDS  
- Timeline: Exposure → AIDS  

**Speaker Notes:**  
"The incubation period, or window period, is 2-12 weeks from infection to detectable antibodies, averaging 4-6 weeks. HIV progresses through acute infection (2-4 weeks with high viral load), clinical latency (8-10 years asymptomatic), and AIDS (CD4 <200 or opportunistic infections). Viral load peaks at 10 million copies/mL during acute phase, then CD4 declines gradually."

**Interactive Element:**

**Technical Directions:**  
- Animation: Clock ticking with phase transitions

## Section 6: Pathophysiology (23:00 - 28:00)

**Visual Suggestion:** Step-by-step viral replication animation. Immune system graphics.

**On-Screen Text:**  
- Replication Cycle: Attachment to Budding  
- Immune Damage: CD4 depletion, dysfunction  
- Stages table  

**Speaker Notes:**  
"HIV's pathophysiology involves attachment via gp120 to CD4 and co-receptors, entry, reverse transcription, integration, and budding. This leads to CD4+ T cell depletion through direct killing, immune activation, and apoptosis. Chronic inflammation causes immune exhaustion and opportunistic infections. Co-receptor switching from CCR5 to CXCR4 marks rapid progression."

**Interactive Element:**

## Section 7: Clinical Features (Dermatological Emphasis) (28:00 - 36:00)

**Visual Suggestion:** High-quality images of skin conditions (with patient consent noted). Dermatological atlas references.

**On-Screen Text:**  
- Acute: Maculopapular rash  
- Chronic Dermatoses: Seborrheic dermatitis, Kaposi sarcoma  
- CD4 Correlations  
- Diagnostic Clues  

**Speaker Notes:**  
"Dermatological manifestations are crucial for HIV diagnosis. Acute infection presents with maculopapular rash on trunk and extremities. Common conditions include seborrheic dermatitis (greasy scales on scalp/face), Kaposi sarcoma (purple/red lesions from HHV-8), oral hairy leukoplakia (EBV-associated tongue patches), herpes infections (chronic ulcers, multidermatomal zoster), fungal infections (candidiasis, extensive tinea), and bacterial folliculitis. CD4 count correlates: >500 shows seborrheic dermatitis, 200-500 Kaposi sarcoma, <200 disseminated infections. Multiple concurrent skin conditions with poor treatment response suggest HIV."

## Section 8: Complications (36:00 - 41:00)

**Visual Suggestion:** Icons for opportunistic infections, malignancies. Indian context statistics.

**On-Screen Text:**  
- Opportunistic Infections: PCP, TB, Cryptococcal  
- Malignancies: Kaposi sarcoma, NHL  
- Neurological/Metabolic complications

**Speaker Notes:**  
"Complications include opportunistic infections like Pneumocystis pneumonia, tuberculosis (often extrapulmonary), cryptococcal meningitis, and toxoplasmosis. Malignancies include Kaposi sarcoma and non-Hodgkin lymphoma. Neurological issues involve dementia and neuropathy. Metabolic complications include lipodystrophy and dyslipidemia. In India, TB-HIV co-infection affects 10% of HIV patients, with malnutrition and mental health issues common."

## Section 9: Diagnosis (41:00 - 46:00)

**Visual Suggestion:** Lab equipment animations, diagnostic flowcharts.

**On-Screen Text:**  
- Tests: Antibody, Viral Load, CD4  
- WHO Staging  
- Algorithm flowchart  

**Speaker Notes:**  
"Diagnosis starts with HIV antibody testing via ELISA screening and Western blot confirmation. Rapid tests provide results in 15-20 minutes. Viral load measures HIV RNA copies/mL for monitoring. CD4 count assesses immune status. WHO clinical staging ranges from asymptomatic to AIDS-defining conditions. Algorithm: Suspected HIV → Rapid test → Confirmation → Staging with CD4/viral load."

## Section 10: Treatment (46:00 - 51:00)

**Visual Suggestion:** ART regimen graphics, adherence charts.

**On-Screen Text:**  
- ART Classes: NRTIs, NNRTIs, PIs, INSTIs  
- NACO Regimens: TLD (first-line)  
- Monitoring: Viral load, adherence  

**Speaker Notes:**  
"Antiretroviral therapy aims for viral suppression and immune reconstitution. NACO recommends first-line Tenofovir + Lamivudine + Dolutegravir (TLD). Classes include NRTIs (Tenofovir), NNRTIs (Efavirenz), PIs (Lopinavir), and INSTIs (Dolutegravir). Monitor viral load every 6 months, target undetectable (<50 copies/mL). Adherence of 95% is crucial. Complications include IRIS and drug resistance."
