Case Study: 25-year-old Female with Genital Symptoms

Patient History: A 25-year-old unmarried female presents with vaginal discharge and lower abdominal pain for 1 week. She reports unprotected sexual intercourse with a new partner 2 weeks ago. No history of similar complaints. Lives in Delhi, India.

Clinical Findings: Yellowish vaginal discharge, lower abdominal tenderness, cervical motion tenderness. No fever. Urine pregnancy test negative.

What diagnostic tests would you order first?
Gram stain and culture
NAAT/PCR testing
Clinical diagnosis only

Correct!

Gram-negative diplococci on gram stain suggest Neisseria gonorrhoeae. Culture confirms diagnosis.

Indian Context: Gonorrhea prevalence high in urban areas. Co-infection with Chlamydia common (30-50%). Drug resistance emerging.

Also Correct!

NAAT/PCR is highly sensitive for gonorrhea detection, especially from urine or vaginal swabs.

Indian Context: Molecular testing becoming more available in urban centers. Useful for asymptomatic screening.

Incorrect

Clinical diagnosis alone is unreliable. Laboratory confirmation essential for proper treatment.

How would you confirm Chlamydia diagnosis?
NAAT/PCR for Chlamydia
Culture
Serology

Correct!

NAAT/PCR is the gold standard for Chlamydia trachomatis detection.

Indian Context: Chlamydia often asymptomatic. High prevalence among young adults. Screening recommended for high-risk groups.

Incorrect

Chlamydia culture is technically difficult and not routinely used. NAAT is preferred.

Incorrect

Serology cannot distinguish current from past infection. Not useful for acute diagnosis.

What clinical feature suggests Bacterial Vaginosis?
Positive whiff test
Clue cells on wet mount
pH >4.5

Correct!

Positive whiff test (fishy odor with KOH) is characteristic of Bacterial Vaginosis.

Indian Context: BV common in reproductive age women. Associated with adverse pregnancy outcomes.

Also Correct!

Clue cells (epithelial cells studded with bacteria) are pathognomonic for BV.

Also Correct!

Vaginal pH >4.5 supports BV diagnosis. Normal vaginal pH is 3.8-4.5.

What would confirm Candidiasis?
KOH mount showing hyphae
Fungal culture
Symptoms alone

Correct!

KOH preparation showing pseudohyphae/pseudomycelia confirms Candidiasis.

Indian Context: Vaginal candidiasis very common. Often recurrent. Diabetes and antibiotic use are risk factors.

Also Correct!

Fungal culture can identify species and confirm diagnosis.

Incorrect

While symptoms are suggestive, microscopic confirmation is needed for accurate diagnosis.
