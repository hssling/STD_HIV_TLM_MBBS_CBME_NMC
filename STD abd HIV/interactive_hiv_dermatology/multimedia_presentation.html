Common HIV Dermatological Conditions
Focus on Indian Epidemiology
🧴
Seborrheic Dermatitis
Most common HIV dermatosis in India. Red scaly patches in skin folds. Exacerbated by tropical climate.
🦗
Pruritic Papular Eruption
Often misdiagnosed as scabies. CD4 <200. Itchy papules on extensor surfaces.
🎭
Kaposi's Sarcoma
HHV-8 associated. Violaceous patches/plaques. 10-20% of Indian AIDS patients.
🍄
Oral Candidiasis
First sign of immunosuppression. White patches on tongue/buccal mucosa.

HIV Dermatology in Indian Context
Cultural, Epidemiological & Healthcare Considerations
🌡️
Tropical Climate Impact
Seborrheic dermatitis exacerbated by heat and humidity. Higher incidence in coastal regions.
🕉️
Cultural Beliefs
Integration of traditional medicine. Addressing stigma and misconceptions about HIV skin conditions.
🏥
NACO Guidelines
Free ART, chemotherapy for Kaposi's sarcoma. Integrated HIV-dermatology clinics in medical colleges.
🔍
Diagnostic Challenges
Limited access to advanced diagnostics. Reliance on clinical acumen and basic investigations.
Clinical Case Studies
Real-world scenarios from Indian HIV clinics

Case 1: Rural HIV Patient with Skin Rash

Patient: 42-year-old farmer, recently diagnosed HIV, CD4 150

Presentation: Itchy red patches in groin and axillae

Challenge: Limited access to dermatologist, traditional medicine beliefs

Diagnosis: Seborrheic Dermatitis

Management: Ketoconazole 2% cream + hydrocortisone, ART optimization, patient education

Outcome: Significant improvement in 2 weeks, continued follow-up
