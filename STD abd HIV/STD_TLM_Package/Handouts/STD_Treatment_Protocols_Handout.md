## Treatment Principles

### Syndromic Management Approach
- **Rationale**: Many STDs have overlapping symptoms; laboratory tests not always available
- **Advantages**: Immediate treatment, prevents complications, reduces transmission
- **Indian Context**: Primary healthcare level; resource-limited settings
- **Limitations**: Overtreatment possible; misses asymptomatic infections

### Essential Drug List (WHO)
- **Bacterial STDs**: Penicillin, ceftriaxone, azithromycin, doxycycline
- **Viral STDs**: Acyclovir, antiretroviral drugs
- **Supportive**: Analgesics, antipyretics, topical agents

### Treatment Considerations
- **Partner Treatment**: Essential for bacterial STDs
- **Pregnancy**: Drug safety assessment
- **Drug Interactions**: Especially with ART
- **Resistance Patterns**: Regional variations in India

## Bacterial STD Treatment Protocols

### Syphilis Treatment (NACO 2020)

| Stage | Recommended Regimen | Alternative | Follow-up |
|-------|-------------------|-------------|-----------|
| **Primary/Secondary** | Benzathine penicillin G 2.4 MU IM single dose | Doxycycline 100mg PO twice daily × 14 days | Clinical + serological at 3, 6, 12 months |
| **Latent (<1 year)** | Benzathine penicillin G 2.4 MU IM single dose | Doxycycline 100mg PO twice daily × 14 days | Serological monitoring |
| **Late Latent (>1 year)** | Benzathine penicillin G 2.4 MU IM weekly × 3 doses | Doxycycline 100mg PO twice daily × 28 days | Long-term follow-up |
| **Neurosyphilis** | Aqueous penicillin G 3-4 MU IV q4h × 14 days | Ceftriaxone 2g IV daily × 14 days | CSF examination, clinical monitoring |
| **Pregnancy** | Benzathine penicillin G (safe all trimesters) | Erythromycin (if allergic) | Fetal monitoring |

**Key Points**:
- **Jarisch-Herxheimer Reaction**: Fever/chills post-treatment; manage supportively
- **Test of Cure**: 4-fold decline in VDRL titer
- **Partner Treatment**: All sexual partners in past 3 months

### Gonorrhea Treatment (NACO 2020)

| Category | Recommended Regimen | Rationale |
|----------|-------------------|-----------|
| **Uncomplicated** | Ceftriaxone 500mg IM single dose + Azithromycin 1g PO single dose | Dual therapy covers chlamydia co-infection |
| **Complicated** | Ceftriaxone 1g IV/IM daily × 7-14 days + Azithromycin 1g PO single dose | Systemic infection |
| **Pharyngeal** | Ceftriaxone 500mg IM single dose + Azithromycin 1g PO single dose | Higher treatment failure rates |
| **Pregnancy** | Ceftriaxone 500mg IM single dose + Azithromycin 1g PO single dose | Safe in pregnancy |

**Key Points**:
- **Test of Cure**: Recommended 7-14 days post-treatment
- **Resistance**: Emerging resistance to ciprofloxacin (not recommended)
- **Partner Treatment**: Essential; expedited partner therapy allowed

### Chlamydia Treatment (NACO 2020)

| Category | Recommended Regimen | Alternative | Duration |
|----------|-------------------|-------------|----------|
| **Uncomplicated** | Azithromycin 1g PO single dose | Doxycycline 100mg PO twice daily | 7 days |
| **Complicated** | Doxycycline 100mg PO twice daily | Azithromycin 1g PO weekly | 14-21 days |
| **Pregnancy** | Azithromycin 1g PO single dose | Amoxicillin 500mg PO three times daily | 7 days |
| **LGV** | Doxycycline 100mg PO twice daily | Erythromycin 500mg PO four times daily | 21 days |

**Key Points**:
- **Test of Cure**: Recommended for high-risk cases or persistent symptoms
- **Partner Treatment**: All partners in past 60 days
- **Re-testing**: 3 months after treatment

## Viral STD Treatment Protocols

### HIV/AIDS Treatment (NACO 2023)

#### First-Line ART Regimens
- **Preferred**: Tenofovir + Lamivudine + Dolutegravir (TLD) - single pill daily
- **Alternative**: Tenofovir + Lamivudine + Efavirenz (TLE) - single pill daily
- **When to Start**: Immediate initiation regardless of CD4 count (test and treat)
- **Monitoring**: Viral load at 6 months, then annually; CD4 every 6 months

#### Second-Line Regimens
- **Indication**: Treatment failure (viral load >1,000 copies/mL after 6 months)
- **Regimen**: Protease inhibitor-based (LPV/r + 2 NRTIs)
- **Monitoring**: Drug resistance testing before switch

#### Special Populations
- **Pregnancy**: TLD safe; additional AZT during labor
- **TB Co-infection**: Rifampicin-compatible regimens (EFV instead of DTG)
- **Children**: Age-appropriate formulations and dosing
- **Hepatitis Co-infection**: Tenofovir preferred (active against HBV)

**Key Points**:
- **Adherence**: 95% target for viral suppression
- **Drug Interactions**: Rifampicin reduces DTG levels
- **Resistance**: Baseline testing in high-risk groups

### Herpes Simplex Virus (CDC 2021)

| Category | Recommended Regimen | Duration |
|----------|-------------------|----------|
| **First Episode** | Acyclovir 400mg PO three times daily | 7-10 days |
| **Recurrent** | Acyclovir 400mg PO three times daily | 5 days |
| **Suppressive** | Acyclovir 400mg PO twice daily | Daily |
| **Severe Disease** | Acyclovir 5-10mg/kg IV three times daily | Until clinical improvement |

**Key Points**:
- **Pregnancy**: Acyclovir safe; suppressive therapy reduces transmission
- **HIV Co-infection**: Higher doses required
- **Resistance**: Rare; foscarnet for resistant cases

### Hepatitis B (AASLD 2021)

| Phase | Treatment Indication | Preferred Regimen |
|-------|-------------------|-------------------|
| **Acute** | Supportive care only | No antivirals |
| **Chronic (HBeAg+)** | HBV DNA >2,000 IU/mL + ALT elevation | Tenofovir or entecavir |
| **Chronic (HBeAg-)** | HBV DNA >2,000 IU/mL + ALT elevation | Tenofovir or entecavir |
| **Decompensated Cirrhosis** | Any detectable HBV DNA | Tenofovir + entecavir |
| **Pregnancy** | High viral load | Tenofovir (safe) |

**Key Points**:
- **Duration**: Long-term (years); HBsAg loss rare
- **Monitoring**: HBV DNA, ALT, HBeAg every 3-6 months
- **Resistance**: Low with current drugs
- **HBV-HIV**: Tenofovir covers both

## Monitoring and Follow-up

### Laboratory Monitoring

#### HIV
- **Baseline**: CD4, viral load, LFT, RFT, CBC, lipid profile
- **Every 6 months**: CD4, viral load
- **Annual**: LFT, RFT, lipid profile, glucose
- **As needed**: Drug resistance, HLA-B*5701 (for ABC hypersensitivity)

#### Syphilis
- **Clinical Follow-up**: 3, 6, 12 months
- **Serological**: VDRL titers (4-fold decline indicates cure)
- **Pregnancy**: Monthly VDRL until delivery

#### Other STDs
- **Gonorrhea/Chlamydia**: Test of cure 7-14 days post-treatment
- **HSV**: Clinical monitoring; no routine lab follow-up
- **HBV**: HBV DNA, ALT every 3-6 months on treatment

### Clinical Follow-up Protocols

#### Short-term (1-2 weeks)
- **Symptoms Resolution**: Assess treatment response
- **Adverse Reactions**: Drug side effects monitoring
- **Partner Treatment**: Confirm completion

#### Medium-term (1-3 months)
- **Re-testing**: For gonorrhea, chlamydia, syphilis
- **HIV Viral Load**: Confirm suppression
- **Adherence Assessment**: Pill count, self-report

#### Long-term (6-12 months)
- **HIV**: Annual monitoring if stable
- **HBV**: Treatment response assessment
- **Cancer Screening**: Cervical cancer for HPV

## Drug Resistance in India

### Bacterial STDs
- **Gonorrhea**: Resistance to ciprofloxacin (90%), penicillin (50%), tetracycline (30%)
- **Syphilis**: Rare resistance; treatment failures reported
- **Chlamydia**: Emerging resistance to azithromycin in some regions

### Viral STDs
- **HIV**: Transmitted resistance in 5-10% of new diagnoses
- **HSV**: Rare; mostly in immunocompromised patients
- **HBV**: Low resistance with tenofovir/entecavir

### Management Strategies
- **Surveillance**: National antimicrobial resistance network
- **Guidelines Update**: Regular revision based on resistance patterns
- **Alternative Drugs**: Stockpiling for resistant cases
- **Prevention**: Appropriate antibiotic use

---

## Treatment Challenges in India

### Adherence Barriers
- **Stigma**: Fear of discrimination affects clinic visits
- **Migration**: Labor migrants lose continuity of care
- **Side Effects**: Gastrointestinal, neurological symptoms
- **Cost**: Transportation, lost wages

### Drug Supply Issues
- **Stockouts**: Occasional ART drug shortages
- **Quality**: Ensuring cold chain for vaccines
- **Counterfeits**: Risk in private sector
- **Procurement**: Centralized NACO system

### Co-infections
- **TB-HIV**: 10% of HIV patients; drug interactions
- **HIV-HBV**: Tenofovir covers both; monitoring complex
- **HIV-STI**: Increased transmission and complications

### Special Populations
- **Pregnancy**: Safe drugs; vertical transmission prevention
- **Children**: Age-appropriate formulations
- **Elderly**: Drug interactions, comorbidities
- **IDUs**: Harm reduction plus treatment

## Integration with Primary Healthcare

### Syndromic Management at PHC Level
- **Urethral Discharge**: Ceftriaxone + azithromycin
- **Vaginal Discharge**: Metronidazole + azithromycin
- **Genital Ulcer**: Ceftriaxone + azithromycin + acyclovir
- **Lower Abdominal Pain**: Ceftriaxone + doxycycline + metronidazole

### Referral Systems
- **Primary Level**: Syndromic treatment
- **Secondary Level**: Laboratory confirmation, complications
- **Tertiary Level**: Specialized care, drug resistance

### Training and Capacity Building
- **Healthcare Providers**: STD management training
- **Counselors**: Adherence and psychosocial support
- **Community Workers**: Outreach and follow-up

---

## Key Facts for MBBS Students
- Treatment protocols must be updated regularly for resistance
- Partner treatment is crucial for bacterial STDs
- ART is lifelong but prevents transmission when viral load suppressed
- Monitoring ensures treatment success and detects failures
- Integration with general healthcare improves access
