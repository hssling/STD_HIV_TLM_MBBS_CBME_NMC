## 1. Etiology

### Causative Agent
- **HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus)**: A retrovirus belonging to the Lentivirus family
- **Two main types**:
  - HIV-1: Most common worldwide, more virulent, causes majority of global infections
  - HIV-2: Predominantly in West Africa, less transmissible, slower progression

### Viral Structure
- **Envelope**: Derived from host cell membrane, contains gp120 and gp41 surface proteins
- **Core**: Contains viral RNA, reverse transcriptase, integrase, and protease enzymes
- **Genome**: Two copies of single-stranded RNA, encodes 9 genes (gag, pol, env, tat, rev, nef, vif, vpr, vpu)

### Key Facts
- HIV targets CD4+ T lymphocytes, macrophages, and dendritic cells
- Uses CD4 receptor and co-receptors (CCR5/CXCR4) for cell entry
- Reverse transcriptase enzyme converts viral RNA to DNA for integration into host genome

## 2. Epidemiology

### Global Burden
- **WHO Estimates (2023)**: 39 million people living with HIV (PLHIV)
- **New infections**: 1.3 million annually
- **AIDS-related deaths**: 630,000 annually
- **Regional distribution**: Sub-Saharan Africa (25.7 million PLHIV), Asia-Pacific (5.8 million)

### Indian Context
- **Prevalence**: 0.22% (NACO 2023)
- **Total PLHIV**: 23.1 lakh (2.31 million)
- **Regional variations**:
  - Highest in Northeast: Nagaland (1.5%), Manipur (1.4%)
  - South India: Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana
  - Urban vs Rural: Higher in urban areas (0.3% vs 0.2%)
- **High-risk groups**:
  - Men who have sex with men (MSM): 17% HIV prevalence
  - Female sex workers (FSW): 2.1%
  - People who inject drugs (PWID): 7.1%
  - Transgender persons: 3.1%

### Transmission Statistics
| Route | Global Contribution | Indian Context |
|-------|-------------------|----------------|
| Heterosexual | 54% | 87% |
| Homosexual | 18% | 2.1% |
| Injecting drug use | 6% | 3.2% |
| Mother-to-child | 4% | 0.9% |
| Blood transfusion | <1% | <0.1% |

### Key Epidemiological Indicators
- **Incidence**: New infections per year
- **Prevalence**: Total existing cases
- **Mortality rate**: AIDS-related deaths
- **ART coverage**: 80% in India (NACO 2023)

## 3. Transmission

### Primary Modes
1. **Sexual Transmission** (most common):
   - Unprotected vaginal, anal, or oral intercourse
   - Higher risk with multiple partners, STIs, or genital ulcers
   - MSM transmission: 17% prevalence in India

2. **Blood-borne Transmission**:
   - Sharing contaminated needles/syringes
   - Blood transfusions (rare in screened blood)
   - Occupational exposure (needle-stick injuries)

3. **Mother-to-Child Transmission (MTCT)**:
   - During pregnancy, labor, or breastfeeding
   - Without intervention: 15-45% transmission rate
   - With ART: <2% transmission rate

### Risk Factors
- **Biological**: Genital ulcers, STIs, lack of circumcision
- **Behavioral**: Multiple partners, inconsistent condom use (5.2% consistent use in India)
- **Social**: Migration, poverty, stigma, low health literacy
- **Indian-specific**: Son preference, early marriage, cross-border migration

### Prevention of Transmission
- **U=U (Undetectable = Untransmittable)**: Viral suppression prevents sexual transmission
- **Condom use**: 80-95% reduction in HIV transmission
- **ART**: Reduces viral load and transmission risk
- **Harm reduction**: Needle exchange programs for PWID

## 4. Incubation Period

### Window Period
- **Definition**: Time from infection to detectable antibodies
- **Duration**: 2-12 weeks (average 4-6 weeks)
- **HIV-1**: 2-8 weeks
- **HIV-2**: 1-3 months

### Phases of HIV Infection
1. **Acute HIV Infection** (Seroconversion): 2-4 weeks post-exposure
2. **Clinical Latency** (Chronic HIV): Asymptomatic period, 8-10 years average
3. **AIDS**: CD4 <200 cells/μL or opportunistic infections

### Timeline of HIV Progression
```
Exposure → Acute Infection (2-4 weeks) → Seroconversion (4-6 weeks) → Clinical Latency (8-10 years) → AIDS (variable)
```

### Key Facts
- Viral load peaks during acute infection (up to 10 million copies/mL)
- CD4 count drops gradually over years
- Without ART, median time to AIDS: 8-10 years
- With ART, can prevent progression indefinitely

## 5. Pathophysiology

### Viral Replication Cycle
1. **Attachment**: gp120 binds to CD4 and co-receptor
2. **Entry**: Fusion with host cell membrane
3. **Reverse Transcription**: RNA → DNA
4. **Integration**: Viral DNA into host genome
5. **Transcription/Translation**: Viral proteins produced
6. **Assembly/Budding**: New virions released

### Immune System Damage
- **CD4+ T cell depletion**: Direct viral killing and immune activation
- **Immune dysfunction**: Loss of cell-mediated immunity
- **Cytokine dysregulation**: Chronic inflammation
- **Opportunistic infections**: Due to impaired immunity

### Natural History Stages
| Stage | CD4 Count | Viral Load | Clinical Features |
|-------|-----------|------------|-------------------|
| Acute | Normal/High | Very High | Seroconversion illness |
| Chronic | Gradual decline | Variable | Asymptomatic |
| AIDS | <200 | High | Opportunistic infections |

### Key Pathogenic Mechanisms
- **Direct cytopathic effect**: Viral killing of CD4 cells
- **Immune activation**: Chronic inflammation leading to exhaustion
- **Apoptosis**: Programmed cell death of uninfected cells
- **Co-receptor switching**: From CCR5 to CXCR4, associated with rapid progression

## 6. Clinical Features (with Emphasis on Dermatological Aspects)

### Acute HIV Infection (Seroconversion Syndrome)
- **Duration**: 1-6 weeks post-exposure
- **Symptoms**: Fever (80%), rash (70%), lymphadenopathy, fatigue
- **Dermatological features**: Maculopapular rash on trunk and extremities

### Chronic HIV Infection (Asymptomatic Phase)
- **Duration**: 8-10 years average
- **Features**: Usually asymptomatic, persistent generalized lymphadenopathy

### AIDS-Defining Illnesses
- **CD4 <200 cells/μL** or opportunistic infections

### Dermatological Manifestations of HIV

#### Common Skin Conditions
1. **Seborrheic Dermatitis**
   - Most common HIV-associated dermatosis
   - Greasy, scaly patches on scalp, face, chest
   - Caused by Malassezia overgrowth due to immune deficiency

2. **Kaposi Sarcoma**
   - HHV-8 associated vascular tumor
   - Purple/red patches, plaques, nodules
   - Oral involvement common
   - More aggressive in HIV patients

3. **Oral Hairy Leukoplakia**
   - EBV-associated white patches on lateral tongue
   - Cannot be scraped off
   - Marker of immunosuppression

4. **Herpes Infections**
   - HSV: Chronic ulcers, disseminated infection
   - VZV: Severe herpes zoster, multidermatomal

5. **Fungal Infections**
   - Candidiasis: Oral thrush, angular cheilitis
   - Dermatophyte infections: Extensive tinea corporis
   - Cryptococcal meningitis: May present with skin lesions

6. **Bacterial Infections**
   - Staphylococcal folliculitis
   - Bacillary angiomatosis (Bartonella)
   - Impetigo, cellulitis

7. **Drug Reactions**
   - Sulfa drug hypersensitivity
   - Nevirapine rash
   - Stevens-Johnson syndrome

#### Dermatological Diagnostic Clues
- **CD4 Count Correlation**:
  - CD4 >500: Seborrheic dermatitis, tinea infections
  - CD4 200-500: Kaposi sarcoma, herpes zoster
  - CD4 <200: Cryptococcal skin lesions, disseminated infections

- **Key Diagnostic Features**:
  - Atypical presentations of common dermatoses
  - Multiple concurrent skin conditions
  - Poor response to standard treatments
  - Association with systemic symptoms

## 7. Complications

### Opportunistic Infections
- **Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PCP)**
- **Tuberculosis**: Extra-pulmonary, disseminated
- **Cryptococcal meningitis**
- **Toxoplasmosis**: Cerebral
- **Cytomegalovirus (CMV)**: Retinitis, colitis
- **Mycobacterium avium complex (MAC)**

### Malignancies
- **Kaposi sarcoma**: Most common HIV-associated cancer
- **Non-Hodgkin lymphoma**
- **Cervical cancer**: Increased incidence
- **Anal cancer**: In MSM

### Neurological Complications
- **HIV-associated dementia**
- **Peripheral neuropathy**
- **Progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML)**

### Metabolic Complications
- **Lipodystrophy**: Fat redistribution
- **Dyslipidemia**: Increased cardiovascular risk
- **Insulin resistance**: Diabetes mellitus
- **Osteoporosis**: Bone mineral loss

### Indian Context Complications
- **TB-HIV co-infection**: 10% of HIV patients have TB
- **Malnutrition**: Common in advanced disease
- **Mental health**: Depression, anxiety, stigma-related issues

## 8. Diagnosis

### Laboratory Diagnosis
1. **HIV Antibody Testing**
   - ELISA: Screening test
   - Western blot: Confirmatory test
   - Rapid tests: Finger-prick blood, results in 15-20 minutes

2. **Viral Load Testing**
   - Quantitative PCR: Measures HIV RNA copies/mL
   - Used for monitoring treatment response
   - Target: Undetectable (<20-50 copies/mL)

3. **CD4 Count**
   - Flow cytometry: Measures CD4+ T cells/μL
   - Staging and treatment initiation
   - Normal: 500-1500 cells/μL

4. **Drug Resistance Testing**
   - Genotypic: Detects mutations in viral genes
   - Phenotypic: Measures drug susceptibility
   - Recommended before ART initiation

### WHO Clinical Staging System
| Stage | Clinical Features |
|-------|-------------------|
| Stage 1 | Asymptomatic or persistent generalized lymphadenopathy |
| Stage 2 | Weight loss <10%, minor mucocutaneous manifestations |
| Stage 3 | Weight loss >10%, chronic diarrhea, pulmonary TB |
| Stage 4 | AIDS-defining conditions (PCP, KS, cryptococcal meningitis) |

### Diagnostic Algorithm
```
Suspected HIV → Rapid antibody test → If positive → Western blot confirmation → CD4 count + Viral load → Staging
```

## 9. Treatment

### Antiretroviral Therapy (ART)
- **Goal**: Viral suppression, immune reconstitution, prevention of transmission
- **Initiation**: All PLHIV regardless of CD4 count (WHO 2015)
- **Regimens**: Combination of 3 drugs from different classes

### ART Classes and Drugs
1. **Nucleoside Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors (NRTIs)**
   - Tenofovir (TDF), Zidovudine (AZT), Lamivudine (3TC)

2. **Non-Nucleoside Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors (NNRTIs)**
   - Efavirenz (EFV), Nevirapine (NVP)

3. **Protease Inhibitors (PIs)**
   - Lopinavir/ritonavir (LPV/r), Atazanavir (ATV)

4. **Integrase Strand Transfer Inhibitors (INSTIs)**
   - Dolutegravir (DTG), Raltegravir (RAL)

### NACO Recommended Regimens (2023)
- **First-line**: Tenofovir + Lamivudine + Dolutegravir (TLD)
- **Alternative first-line**: Tenofovir + Lamivudine + Efavirenz (TLE)
- **Second-line**: Protease inhibitor-based regimens

### Treatment Monitoring
- **Viral load**: Every 6 months
- **CD4 count**: Every 6 months
- **Adherence**: 95% target for viral suppression
- **Side effects**: Regular assessment

### Complications of ART
- **Immune Reconstitution Inflammatory Syndrome (IRIS)**: Worsening of opportunistic infections
- **Drug resistance**: Due to poor adherence
- **Metabolic side effects**: Dyslipidemia, lipodystrophy
- **Hepatotoxicity**: Especially with nevirapine

## 10. Control and Prevention

### Primary Prevention
- **ABC Approach**: Abstinence, Be faithful, Condoms
- **Vaccines**: Hepatitis B vaccination
- **Pre-exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP)**: Available since 2017 in India
- **Post-exposure Prophylaxis (PEP)**: Within 72 hours of exposure

### Secondary Prevention
- **Early diagnosis**: Through ICTCs (1,381 centers in India)
- **ART initiation**: Prevents progression and transmission
- **Partner notification**: Voluntary or provider-assisted

### Tertiary Prevention
- **Management of opportunistic infections**
- **Rehabilitation services**: Community Care Centers (CCCs)
- **Support groups**: PLHIV networks

### National Programme Linkage

#### National AIDS Control Programme (NACP-VI, 2021-2026)
- **Objectives**: End AIDS by 2030, 95-95-95 targets
- **95-95-95 Targets**:
  - 95% of PLHIV know their status
  - 95% of diagnosed receive ART
  - 95% of on ART achieve viral suppression

#### Key Components
1. **Targeted Interventions (TI)**: For high-risk groups
2. **Condom Promotion**: 800 million condoms distributed annually
3. **Prevention of Parent-to-Child Transmission (PPTCT)**: Covers 95% pregnant women
4. **Blood Safety**: 100% voluntary blood donation
5. **Integrated Counseling and Testing Centers (ICTCs)**: Free HIV testing

#### Achievements (NACO 2023)
- **New infections reduced by 66%** (2007-2017)
- **ART coverage**: 80% of PLHIV
- **MTCT rate**: <2% in PPTCT centers
- **Condom distribution**: 800 million annually

#### Challenges in India
- **Stigma and discrimination**: Deep-rooted cultural barriers
- **MSM and transgender rights**: Legal and social issues
- **Migration**: Interstate labor migration
- **Funding**: Donor-dependent programs
- **Co-infections**: TB-HIV common

### Community-Based Strategies
- **Link Worker Scheme**: Community-level contact tracing
- **Positive People Networks**: PLHIV-led organizations
- **Workplace interventions**: Corporate sector involvement
- **School-based education**: Comprehensive sexuality education
